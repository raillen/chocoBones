# Design Research

## 1. Reference Gathering
Collect references systematically from direct competitors, analogous industries, and established design patterns. Do not rely on subjective preference. Focus on proven patterns for the specific user goal.

## 2. Evidence vs. Inference
Strictly separate observed evidence from derived inference. 'Users abandoned the form' is evidence. 'The form was too long' is an inference. Validate inferences before basing design decisions on them.

## 3. Pattern Extraction
Analyze references to extract atomic design patterns. Identify common structural solutions to specific interaction problems. Document why a pattern works contextually, not just how it looks.

## 4. User Context Analysis
Define the environmental and emotional context of the user. Are they rushed? On mobile? Is the task high-stakes? Design decisions must adapt to these specific contextual pressures.

## 5. Friction Mapping
Identify interaction friction in existing workflows. Map every click, scroll, and cognitive leap required. Set goals to minimize these friction points in the proposed design.

## 6. Competitor Audits
Perform structured heuristic evaluations of competitor products. Document their strengths and weaknesses regarding usability, accessibility, and visual hierarchy.

## 7. Stakeholder Alignment
Research must align with business objectives. Ensure extracted patterns support the specific KPIs of the project (e.g., conversion rate, time-on-task, error reduction).

## 8. Qualitative Synthesis
Synthesize qualitative feedback into actionable insights. Look for recurring themes rather than acting on isolated user complaints. Use affinity mapping to group related observations.

## 9. Best Practice Benchmarking
Benchmark against established Nielsen Norman Group or similar UX heuristics. Ensure foundational usability principles are met before exploring novel interaction paradigms.

## 10. Documentation Standards
Document all research findings clearly with source attribution. A design decision should be traceable back to specific research evidence or established heuristic principles.
